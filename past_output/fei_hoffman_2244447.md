# Professor Fei Hoffman

This briefing provides an evidence-based overview of what to expect from Professor Fei Hoffman based on 34 scraped RateMyProfessors reviews collected between 2018 and 2026.

---

### 1. Snapshot
* **School:** California State University, Long Beach
* **Department:** Computer Engineering / Computer Science
* **Average Quality Rating:** 1.5 / 5
* **Average Difficulty:** 3.1 / 5 (Official overview: 3.1; Sample aggregate: 3.03)
* **Would Take Again:** 17.6% (Official page overview) / 46.2% (Calculated from review sample)
* **Total Reviews Listed:** 32 on RMP (34 total review entries collected in dataset)

---

### 2. Personality and Teaching Style
Reviews describe a passive, hands-off teaching style heavily reliant on pre-recorded videos and online textbook readings:

* **Minimal Direct Lecturing:** Reviews spanning from 2020 through 2026 consistently state that during class sessions, Professor Hoffman plays pre-recorded video lectures (often 10–20 minutes long) while sitting in silence, or reads slides/Zybooks verbatim.
* **Hands-Off Group Management:** In upper-division capstone courses (CECS 491A/B), she operates more like a administrative manager than a technical mentor, letting groups work independently while providing little to no technical assistance.
* **Inflexible Communication:** Multiple reviews describe her as soft-spoken, difficult to negotiate with, and unwilling to adjust grades or acknowledge errors once entered into Canvas. Recent reviews from 2024–2026 mention friction during progress reviews, with students noting she can be overly dismissive or critical during group presentations.

---

### 3. Course Difficulty and Workload
While her overall difficulty rating is moderate (3.1 out of 5), student experiences vary widely depending on the course type:

* **Senior Capstone (491A, 491B, CECS491):** Technical difficulty is set by the student group's chosen project. However, the administrative workload is heavy and tedious, involving extensive documentation, work reports, and monthly presentations.
* **Lower-Division / Lecture Courses (CECS 174, 343, 453):** Workload relies heavily on third-party systems like Zybooks (readings, quizzes, labs) or memorizing lengthy PowerPoint slide decks (over 100 slides) for multiple-choice exams.

### 5. Grading, Exams, and What Tends to Determine the Grade
Despite a low overall quality rating (1.5/5), student grades in the dataset are overwhelmingly high: out of 24 reported grades in the sample, **19 are in the A range** (16 A's, 3 A+'s, 1 A-), alongside 2 B+'s and 2 B's.

* **Heavy Attendance Weight:** Attendance is mandatory in most sections (28 out of 33 reviews with attendance noted) and accounts for up to 30% of the overall final grade in capstone courses, often logged at both the start and end of class.
* **Documentation & Formatting Focus:** Grades on project deliverables heavily depend on strict adherence to instructions, formatting rules, and volume ("quantity over quality"). Students note losing points for minor details like font size or not meeting page length requirements.
* **Exams (Non-Capstone Courses):** In CECS 343 and CECS 174, exams made up 75% of the grade, consisting of multiple-choice questions testing slide memorization.

---

### 6. What to Expect Week to Week
* **In Senior Capstone (CECS 491A / 491B):**
  * **Class Start:** Mandatory attendance call at the beginning of class.
  * **Instruction:** A short pre-recorded lecture video played in class.
  * **Group Work / Deliverables:** Periodic milestone presentations, monthly progress reports, and extensive written documentation.
* **In Standard Lecture Courses (CECS 174 / 343 / 453):**
  * **Class Time:** Reading directly from Zybooks or PowerPoint slides.
  * **Assignments:** Weekly Zybook readings, auto-graded labs, and periodic multiple-choice midterms/finals.

---

### 7. Advice for Students Who Take This Professor
* **Attend Every Class:** Attendance is non-negotiable and represents a major portion of your grade; do not arrive late or leave early.
* **Follow Assignment Instructions Exactly:** Pay extreme attention to detailed formatting guidelines, work report formulas, and document requirements to avoid arbitrary point deductions.
* **Provide Thorough Documentation:** For project classes, write detailed, exhaustive documentation—reviews note that document length and complete submission of required sections heavily influence your score.
* **Be Self-Reliant for Technical Work:** Expect to learn programming languages, frameworks, and tools on your own or with your project team without instructional guidance.
* **Verify Grades Early:** Once a grade is finalized in Canvas, multiple reviews note she is extremely unlikely to change it upon appeal.

---

### 8. Caveats
* **RateMyProfessors Bias:** RMP naturally attracts students with extreme views (either highly dissatisfied or looking to give quick course advice).
* **Grade vs. Satisfaction Disconnect:** There is a stark contrast between her 1.5/5 quality rating and the high proportion of "A" grades reported (19 of 24 reported grades). Dissatisfaction stems primarily from class structure, communication, and teaching methods rather than an inability to pass.
* **Sample Composition:** Out of 34 reviews, 26 focus on senior design capstone courses (491A, 491B, CECS491) up through May 2026. Data for non-capstone courses (CECS 174, 343, 453) comes from small sample sizes (2 to 4 reviews each) dated between 2018 and 2022.
